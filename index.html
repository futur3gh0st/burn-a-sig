Instructions

New Outlook & Outlook on the web
  1. Fill in the boxes on the left and check the preview.
  2. Click Copy signature for Outlook.
  3. In Outlook, open Settings (gear icon) › Accounts › Signatures.
  4. Click New signature, give it a name, click into the editing box and paste (Ctrl+V, or Cmd+V on a Mac).
  5. Set it as the default for New messages and for Replies/forwards, then Save.
Classic Outlook for Windows
  1. Click Copy signature for Outlook.
  2. Go to File › Options › Mail › Signatures.
  3. Click New, give it a name, and paste into the editing box.
  4. Under Choose default signature, set it for New messages and Replies/forwards. Click OK.
Classic Outlook for Mac
  1. Click Copy signature for Outlook.
  2. Go to Outlook › Settings › Signatures.
  3. Click +, give it a name, and paste into the editing box.
  4. Set it as the default for new messages and replies, then close the window.
Outlook on a phone (iPhone, iPad, Android)
  1. Click Copy phone version in the box above.
  2. In the Outlook app, tap your profile picture or initials, top-left.
  3. Tap the gear icon for Settings, then tap Signature.
  4. Delete the text already there (usually "Get Outlook for iOS"), paste yours in, then go back to save.

Do not paste the desktop signature into a phone. It will show unreadable code.

Booking links

The booking link is off by default. Not everyone wants one, so it is opt-in.

  • Paste a Calendly, Microsoft Bookings, Cal.com or similar link into the Booking link field and it appears as a button-style link.
  • Change the wording with Booking link wording — "BOOK A MEETING", "SCHEDULE A CALL", whatever fits.
  • Do not add one on someone else’s behalf without asking. Whether to publish bookable time is the individual’s call.

To remove a link later: open the signature editor, select the words, and delete them.

If the logo and icons do not appear
  • Most often Outlook is waiting for permission to show pictures. Click Download pictures or Show images at the top of the message.
  • Always send yourself a test email and open it on both computer and phone before rolling a signature out.
  • If images are missing for everyone at once, whatever hosts them is unreachable. A logo must sit at a public HTTPS URL that stays put — if it moves or is deleted, every signature already sent loses it.
  • If you opened this page from a file on your computer rather than the web, the preview images will not load, but the copied signature is still correct.
Everything runs in your browser. Nothing you type is uploaded, stored or logged — there is no backend and no analytics. The contact icons are served from this site; your logo is served from wherever you host it.
